1 CLINICAL IMAGE CASE Diagnosis of and treatment for undisplaced complete fractures are complicated. An 80‐year‐old woman previously reported in this journal 1 underwent rigid internal fixation for post‐hip arthroplasty thigh pain due to an incomplete stress fracture above the stem tip. She could ambulate independently with no complications 3 years postoperatively. However, she was hit by a bicycle while walking. This resulted in a direct contusion to the right thigh, where the surgeries were performed. She then visited our hospital for severe right thigh pain. However, radiography and CT showed no apparent fracture (Figure 1A and data not shown). Due to severe pain and gait disturbance, 18F‐sodium fluoride positron emission tomography (NaF PET) was performed. 2 A high intensity surrounding the entire middle portion of her right femur was observed (Figure 1B), indicating the presence of a complete fracture without femur misalignment. The rigid fixation prevented displacement. In this case, strict conservative treatments, such as non‐weight‐bearing exercises for more than 1 month during hospitalization and anti‐sclerostin therapy, effectively alleviated the pain, restored the patient's walking ability, and decreased NaF uptake (Figure 1C). NaF PET scan helped diagnose occult peri‐prosthetic and peri‐implant fractures, even when imaging studies, such as MRI, were not suitable due to artifacts caused by implants. FIGURE 1 Time‐course changes of NaF uptake before and after the accident. (A) Femur radiography, obtained during the patient's first hospital visit after the accident, revealed no apparent fracture. (B) Maximum intensity projection (MIP) image showed a high intensity in the middle of her right femur. Higher magnification images of the framed area are shown in the middle and right panels. (C) NaF PET/CT axial scan, passing horizontally through the body at the level of the middle of the femur. The tracer uptake at the femur center 2 weeks after the accident (middle panel) was higher than that 2 years before the accident (upper panel).
