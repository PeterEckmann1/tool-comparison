Materials and Methods Materials Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, Sigma), KCl, LiCl, sodium acetate, 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium chloride, tris(hydroxymethyl)aminomethane (Tris), and ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) were purchased from Aldrich. The three translocation buffers: 1 mM EDTA, 10 mM Tris, and 1 M KCl; 1 mM EDTA, 10 mM Tris, and 6 M LiCl; and 1 mM EDTA, 10 mM Tris, and 3 M LiCl (pH 8); were used in this work as indicated. Fabrication of nanopore The nanochips (NBPT001Y-HR) used in this work were purchased from Norcada (Canada). SiN x membranes (20 nm in thickness) covered both sides of a silicon substrate and were placed on top of a 10 × 10 μm window. The nanochips were soaked in deionized water and filtered with ethanol for at least 20 min at room temperature to remove the organic and inorganic impurities before use. The nanopore was made on the SiN x membrane using an electric pulse generated using a Keithley 2450 device that was controlled by the labVIEW program as previously described (Kwok et al., 2014; Yin et al., 2017; Acar et al., 2019; Gao et al., 2019; Tian et al., 2019). The pore size was finely adjusted by precisely controlling the applied voltages across the membrane and the step length. The diameter of the nanopores was calculated based on a theoretical derivation under nanopore quasi-1D resistance approximation plus access resistances as follows: G = σ [ ( 4 L / π d 2 ) + ( 1 / d ) ] − 1 (Kowalczyk et al., 2011; Rivas et al., 2018), and transmission electron microscopy (TEM, Tecnai F20, FEI) was used to verify that the physical sizes were close to the calculated values. Here, σ is the electrical conductivity (the value was 10.41 S/m in 1 M KCl, 10 mM Tris, and 1 mM EDTA [pH 8]), L is the length of the nanopore, equal to the thickness of the SiN x membrane (20 nm in this case), G is the pore conductance, and d is the pore diameter. The size of the nanopore used in this work was between 3 and 6 nm. Preparation of xylan samples NP and bs1 native xylans were extracted from the internodes of approximately 20 mature plants using DMSO as previously described (Zhang et al., 2017). NP-ac and bs1-ac xylans were prepared by treating the corresponding native xylan with 1 N NaOH. The rice polysaccharide mixtures were prepared by treating rice cell wall residues with 4 N KOH. meGX, which is made from beech wood and widely used in reported research (Biely et al., 1985; Maceková et al., 2006), was purchased from Sigma (M5144). In addition, the prepared xylan samples were subjected to a monosaccharide composition assay as previously described (Zhang et al., 2019). To determine the acetyl esters in xylan samples, the native xylans were saponified with an equal volume of sodium hydroxide (1 M) and neutralized with an equal volume of hydrogen chloride (1 M). The released acetic acid in the supernatant was quantified using an Acetate Kinase Format Kit (Megazyme) as previously described (Zhang et al., 2017). Measurement of plant polysaccharides The nanopore sensor, in which an SiN x chip that harbors a nanoscale pore was inserted into a cell equipped with Ag/AgCl electrodes, is depicted in Figure 1A. During analysis, the cell was filled with buffer solution (1 mM EDTA, 10 mM Tris, and 6 M LiCl [pH 8]), and the analytes flowing through the nanopore were driven by an electric field applied to the monolayer membrane. In brief, 500 nM polysaccharide samples were mixed in 6 M LiCl buffer solution and loaded into the cis-chamber. A positive or negative transmembrane potential was applied to the nanopore sensor. As xylan molecules translocated through the orifice, the current pulse and blockage duration were determined and recorded. In addition, all the solutions were prepared using HPLC-grade deionized water generated by a water purification system (Molecular 1850D). The zeta potential of the samples was measured on a Zetasizer Nano ZS with an aqueous solution of 5 mg/L. Collection and analysis of data Polysaccharide experiments were conducted with external potentials from 50 to 250 mV. The I-V characteristics and ion current blockade of the samples were recorded on a patch-clamp amplifier (Axopatch 200B) sampled at 25 kHz and low-pass filtered at 5 kHz with sensitive electronics, which were housed inside a Faraday cage. Data analysis was performed on Clampfit and Origin for graphing. All the event spikes were collected at an open pore current of 10%, which was approximately four times larger than the noise; all dwell times longer than 200 ms and normalized current blockages lower than 0.05 were excluded from the data analysis. NMR spectroscopy analysis The NMR characterizations of NP and bs1 xylans were performed as previously described (Zhang et al., 2017). In brief, ∼10 mg of native xylans was dissolved in DMSO-d6 (99.9% D, Sigma) and analyzed using an Agilent DD2 600-MHz NMR spectrometer equipped with a gradient 5-mm 1H/13C/15N triple resonance cold probe, and the measurement time for each sample was 12 h. The spectra were acquired using the Agilent standard pulse sequence gHSQCAD to determine the one-bond 13C-1H correlation. NMR data processing and analysis were conducted using MestReNova 10.0.2 software (Mestrelab Research). Gel permeation chromatography analysis For analysis of the xylan molecule size, xylans (1 mg/mL) were dissolved in mixed solvent (VDMSO:VH2O = 9:1) and filtered before loading onto the Agilent PL-50 system equipped with a column of PLgel Olexis and detected by a differential detector. The xylan molecules were eluted with 1 mL min.−1 DMSO at 35°C. GC-MS analysis For GC-MS analysis, 2 mg of destarched alcohol insoluble residue was hydrolyzed in 2 M trifluoroacetic acid at 121°C for 90 min. The supernatants were air-dried and reduced with sodium borohydride (10 mg/mL in 1 M ammonium hydroxide). The generated alditol acetates were extracted in ethyl acetate and analyzed using an Agilent 7890 GC system equipped with a 5977AMSD (Agilent).
